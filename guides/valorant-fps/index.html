More FPS in VALORANT

VALORANT is light on the graphics card and heavy on the CPU and memory, so the settings that matter are different from most games. Here's what to change, in order of impact, and why.

Updated November 2026

Check your FPS in a real match or Deathmatch, not the Range: the Range runs much faster than a full lobby, so it hides the drops you feel in games.

Fix your PC's setup first

These are worth more than any in-game setting, and they help every game. Most PCs have at least one of them wrong.

  1. Run your monitor at its real refresh rate. Many 144 Hz and 240 Hz monitors run at 60 Hz out of the box, so you never see the extra frames. Settings → System → Display → Advanced display → Choose a refresh rate, and pick the highest.
  2. Plug the monitor into the graphics card. A cable in the motherboard's port makes games run on the processor's weak built-in graphics, or routes every frame through it. Use the ports on the graphics card at the back of the PC.
  3. Turn on XMP or EXPO in the BIOS. Memory runs at a slow default speed until you turn on its profile (XMP on Intel boards, EXPO on AMD). It's one setting, and in CPU-heavy games it's often worth 10–20%. Two matching sticks in the right slots (dual channel) matter even more than speed.
  4. Keep Game Mode on and background recording off. Settings → Gaming → Game Mode: On. Then Settings → Gaming → Captures → Record what happened: Off, so Windows isn't recording in the background while you play.
  5. Update your graphics driver. New drivers fix stutters and add per-game optimizations. Get them from NVIDIA, AMD or Intel directly.
  6. Laptops: plug in and use the real graphics card. On battery, laptops cut power hard. Plug in, then Settings → System → Display → Graphics, pick the game, and set it to High performance so it runs on the graphics card, not the built-in chip.

Why VALORANT is CPU-bound

Even a mid-range graphics card can push VALORANT past 300 FPS, so the processor and memory decide where you land. That's why turning on XMP/EXPO and running two memory sticks matter so much more here than lowering graphics settings, and why X3D processors like the Ryzen 7 9800X3D lead every VALORANT benchmark.

Questions

What's the single best setting for FPS in VALORANT?

Multithreaded Rendering: On. It spreads VALORANT's work over more CPU cores, and since VALORANT is limited by the CPU on most PCs, it's worth more than any graphics setting.

Why is my FPS still low in VALORANT?

Usually a setup problem rather than a setting: a monitor running at 60 Hz, memory at its slow default speed, one memory stick, or the monitor plugged into the motherboard. Fix those first; they're worth more than any in-game setting.
